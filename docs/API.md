# Public API

## Any diagram, through one path

- `MermaidDiagramPreparation.prepare(source:typography:limits:narrowestColumnWidth:now:timeZone:)` reads a diagram's text, lays it out and returns a `PreparedMermaidDiagram`, whatever its type, or throws a `MermaidDiagramPreparationFailure` with the error that refused it (`reading`, a type's layout error, `nothingToDraw`, `tooLargeToDraw`) and its `category`: `unreadable` (a syntax problem and its line, or no type keyword), `typeNotDrawn` (its keyword), `notDrawnYet` (statements or options its type does not draw yet), `beyondLimits` and `refusedContent` (links in a circle, a date not in its format, no series). `prepare(_:typography:…)` takes a `MermaidDiagram` already read.
- `PreparedMermaidDiagram`: its `kind` (`MermaidDiagramKind`, with Pele's name of the type and the name a screen reader says), its `scene`, its `narrowScene` for a column narrower than it: the diagram as Pele draws it for a container as wide as `narrowestColumnWidth` (`standardNarrowestColumnWidth`, 280 points, by default; any width, so a reader prepares again at its column's real width, as Obsidian draws again for its container), by the type's own use of Pele's `fit-width.ts` (`DiagramWidthFitting`): turned to run down under the 640-point breakpoint (a flowchart, state, class, entity relationship, requirement, use case, swimlane or event model diagram, an agent flow, a Git graph, a timeline, a mindmap, a user journey, an Ishikawa diagram, a packet diagram), drawn tight (a flowchart, class, sequence, C4, block, architecture, railroad or swimlane diagram, a tree view), the plot fitted to the width (a pie, radar, Sankey or Wardley chart), or the page capped to it (a Gantt, quadrant, XY chart, treemap); `PeleNarrowDifferentialTests` compares it with Pele's drawing at 280, 390 and 600 points, which a column shows by `showsNarrowScene(inColumnWidth:scale:)` or `scene(inColumnWidth:scale:)`; it is laid out the first time it is read, from any thread, and kept, and those two read it only for a column the regular scene is wider than, so a diagram shown only in columns it fits never lays it out (a reader that reads `narrowScene` itself before asking lays it out at that read), its `accessibility` (`MermaidDiagramAccessibility`: the `label`, its title or the type's name, and the `value`, its description or a summary of what it shows), its `unappliedStatementCount`, and `svgDocument(colors:identifierPrefix:)`.
- `DiagramScene`: a laid-out diagram's painting, whatever its type: its page `size`, its `font`, and its `items` back to front, each a `DiagramSceneShape` (one `CGPath` filled, stroked with a `DiagramStroke`, its width, dashes, caps and joins, filled and then stroked as an outlined box is, or filled with a `DiagramLinearGradient`) or a `DiagramSceneText` (a measured `DiagramTextBlock`, bold, italic, monospaced or turned, its `lines` placed as they are drawn, and its `styledRuns` when pieces of a line are bold, italic or monospaced: each a `DiagramTextRun` with its `DiagramTextStyle` and width, one after the other from the line's start). Every color is a `DiagramPaint`: a `DiagramPaletteRole` of `DiagramPalette` (background, foreground, line, surface, border, accent, critical, muted, alternate surface, series), a mixture of two roles, or a color the diagram's text set, at an opacity. Paints stay roles until they are drawn, so a theme change paints the same scene again. Shapes painted alike and apart are merged, so a view paints as few shapes as possible; each item has the `DiagramScenePart` Pele's SVG names it by. `DiagramScene(layout:)` makes the scene of any type's layout. The scene keeps its shapes as path elements in one block: `itemCount` and `forEachItem(_:)` read them where they lie (a `DiagramSceneShapeView` gives its elements or `makePath()`), while each read of `items` or `shapes` makes every path anew, so a view reads them once. `storageIdentifier` tells a scene and its copies from every other scene alive, for a host that keys what it made from a scene, such as a picture.
- `DiagramSeriesColors` (with `DiagramThemeColor`): the series colors a diagram's theme variables set and Pele's label inks on them ([Theme palettes](Diagram-types.md#theme-palettes)); `DiagramScene.seriesColors` carries them and `DiagramScene.palette(over:)` gives the host's palette with them in place, which a host that paints the scene with its own shapes paints the scene's series roles in.
- `DiagramSceneRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` draws any scene into a `CGContext` whose y runs down, its series those its theme variables set over the palette's. Every type's own renderer (`FlowchartRenderer` and the others below) paints its scene this way.
- `DiagramSVGWriter(colors:identifierPrefix:paintsBackground:).document(of:diagramType:title:description:)` writes any scene as a standalone SVG 1.1 document in Pele's structure (`class="pele pele-<type>"`, a `path` per shape and a `text` per text with Pele's class for its part, `title` and `desc` for screen readers), its colors either a palette's (`DiagramSVGColors.palette`) or Pele's CSS variables with Pele's colors as fallbacks (`.peleVariables`: `var(--pele-bg,#fff)`, `color-mix` for mixtures), so that an Obsidian theme colors it as it colors Pele's. Every text is escaped; nothing is a script or refers outside the document.

## Each type

- `MermaidDiagramReader.read(_:limits:)` reads a diagram's text and returns a `MermaidDiagram` (one case per type read), or throws a `MermaidDiagramReadError`: no diagram, a type not read yet (by its keyword), a syntax problem with its line (`MermaidSyntaxProblem`), or a limit passed.
- `MermaidDiagramLimits`: the bounds a diagram is read and laid out within.
- `FlowchartLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:limits:)` lays a flowchart out and returns a `FlowchartLayout`: its size, and each node's shape, frame and text, each edge's path, marks and text, and the title and the groups' boxes (`FlowchartLayoutCluster`), on a page whose y runs down. It throws `FlowchartLayoutError` past the layout limit (`subgraphsNotLaidOut` is kept for callers and thrown only for groups inside each other).
- `DiagramTypography(font:)`: the measurers of a `DiagramFont` (the system font or one by PostScript name), made once and used for every diagram in that font. `CoreTextDiagramTextMeasurer` measures with Core Text; `DiagramTextMeasuring` is what the layout measures with.
- `DiagramTextBlock`, `DiagramPath` (with `DiagramPathSegment`) and `DiagramEdgeMark`: placed text, lines and edge marks.
- `DiagramWidthFitting` is Pele's `common/fit-width.ts` as value-type helpers: `fitWidth` (a chart whose plot gives up what its labels overflow, up to three redraws), `capWidth`, `turnToFit` (across, or down under the 640-point breakpoint when that is narrower), `tighten` and `titleRoom`, each taking the width the container has (nil, zero or less for none). The layout engines of the types that narrow take an optional `availableWidth` (`MindmapLayoutEngine`, `QuadrantChartLayoutEngine`, `XYChartLayoutEngine`, `SankeyLayoutEngine`, `GanttLayoutEngine`) whose default nil is the layout of a container without a limit, and `UserJourneyLayoutEngine.columnLayout(_:typography:availableWidth:)` gives a journey's column of cards or nil; `MermaidDiagramPreparation` makes `narrowScene` with them.
- The options a diagram's front matter and directives set are read as Pele's YAML reader reads them (block mappings, flow mappings and `config: {…}` in the front matter, a directive's JSON, options inside an option such as an axis' or `nodeColors`; plain values with js-yaml's spellings, so `False`, `NULL`, `0x1F` and `.inf` are a Boolean, nothing and numbers) and kept per type: `PieChartOptions`, `GitGraphOptions`, `GanttOptions`, `QuadrantChartOptions`, `XYChartOptions` (with `XYChartAxisOptions`) and `SankeyOptions` (with `SankeyLinkColor`), each a property of its diagram.
- `FlowchartDrawingParts(layout:)` groups a layout's geometry by how it is painted: Core Graphics paths for the groups' boxes (`FlowchartClusterDrawingParts`: fills by depth, outlines, titles, and the patches of titles edges cross), the solid, thick and dotted edges, the filled and stroked marks, the patches behind edge text, the node fills and the node outlines, the shapes painted piece by piece (`layeredNodeShapes`, `FlowchartShapeLayer` with its `FlowchartShapeLayerPainting`; a styled node's in `FlowchartStyledNodeParts.layers`), with the text blocks; a view paints them with its own shapes and colors.
- `FlowchartRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints the flowchart's scene, made from those parts, into a `CGContext` whose y runs down, in a `DiagramPalette` (Pele's color roles: background, foreground, line, surface, border, alternate surface for boxes, muted for their titles). It parses and measures nothing, so a change of colors only draws again.
- `MindmapLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` lays a mindmap out (`MindmapLayout`: each node's outline, frame, text and branch, each link's path and branch, the title); `MindmapDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by branch for painting, and `MindmapRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it, each branch in `DiagramPalette.seriesColor(_:)`. `MindmapDiagram`, `MindmapNode` and `MindmapNodeShape` are a mindmap as its text describes it.
- `TimelineLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` lays a timeline out (`TimelineLayout`: sections, periods with their dots, lines and events, the axis and its arrow, the title); `TimelineDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by color, and `TimelineRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it. `TimelineDiagram`, `TimelinePeriod` and `TimelineDirection` are a timeline as its text describes it.
- `FlowchartDiagram`, `FlowchartNode` (with `FlowchartNodeAsset` for a node drawn as an icon or a picture), `FlowchartNodeShape`, `FlowchartEdge` (with `FlowchartEdgeStroke` and `FlowchartArrowhead`), `FlowchartSubgraph` (its nodes, nested groups, direction and the node standing for it), `FlowchartDirection`: a flowchart as its text describes it.
- `DiagramLabel`: the text of a node, an edge or a group as lines, read as Pele's `layoutLabel` reads it (`<br>`, a written `\n` and line breaks end lines; Markdown's and HTML's emphasis become bold, italic and monospaced pieces; other tags are dropped; `fa:fa-…` icon names are left out, as Pele leaves out icons it has no drawing for; entity codes are read as Pele reads them; spaces are kept), each line drawn as the characters it holds. Lines of a label of more than one are 1.3 times the size apart, a single line 1.5.
- `StateDiagramLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:limits:)` lays a state diagram out and returns a `StateDiagramLayout`: its size, each state's kind, frame, name and other descriptions, each transition's path, arrow and text, each note's frame, text and line to its state, the composite states' boxes (`composites`, drawn as a flowchart's groups, each name centred in a band along its top with a line under the band, as Pele draws it) and the dashed lines between regions, and the title. Transitions meet their states where Pele's layout spread and settled their ends. It throws `StateDiagramLayoutError` past the layout limit (`compositeStatesNotLaidOut` is kept for callers and thrown only for composites inside each other).
- `StateDiagramDrawingParts(layout:)` groups a state diagram's geometry by how it is painted, and `StateDiagramRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it into a `CGContext`, as the flowchart's do; a note's text is `DiagramPalette.noteText`, between the background and the text.
- `StateDiagram`, `StateDiagramState` (with `StateDiagramStateKind`), `StateDiagramTransition`, `StateDiagramNote` (with `StateDiagramNoteSide`), `StateDiagramComposite`: a state diagram as its text describes it.
- `ClassDiagramLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:limits:)` lays a class diagram out and returns a `ClassDiagramLayout`: each class's box with its compartment lines, annotations, name and members, the notes, the interfaces' names, each relation's path, marks, text and cardinalities, the namespaces' boxes (`namespaces`, drawn as a flowchart's groups), and the title. It throws `ClassDiagramLayoutError` past the layout limit. `ClassDiagramDrawingParts(layout:)` groups its geometry by how it is painted, and `ClassDiagramRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it in a `ClassDiagramPalette` (a `DiagramPalette` and Pele's muted and note colors).
- `ClassDiagram`, `ClassDiagramClass`, `ClassDiagramMember` (with `ClassDiagramMemberClassifier`), `ClassDiagramRelation` (with `ClassDiagramRelationEndpoint`, `ClassDiagramRelationEnd` and `ClassDiagramLineStyle`), `ClassDiagramNote` and `ClassDiagramNamespace`: a class diagram as its text describes it, with `hierarchicalNamespaces` from its front matter.
- `SequenceLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` lays a `SequenceDiagram` out and returns a `SequenceLayout`: the boxes (`SequenceLayoutBox`), the participants above and (but the destroyed) below their lifelines in their shapes (`SequenceParticipantKind`), a created one at the message that creates it, the lifelines, highlights, activations, block frames, messages with their heads (half heads with their barb's side), numbers and central connections, the crosses of destroyed lifelines, the notes and the title. `SequenceLayoutError` and `SequenceDiagram.statementsNotLaidOut` (with `SequenceStatementNotLaidOut`) are kept for callers that name them and are no longer produced: every statement read is laid out. `SequenceDrawingParts(layout:)` and `SequenceRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paint it as the flowchart's parts and renderer do, with Pele's muted text and second surface mixed between the palette's background and foreground, and, as Pele, its dark or white text (`SequenceTextInk`) for a box's title, a figure's text and a message's line and text on a colored box or `rect`.
- `SequenceDiagram`, `SequenceParticipant` (with `SequenceParticipantKind`), `SequenceBox`, `SequenceEvent`, `SequenceMessage` (with `SequenceArrow`, `SequenceArrowhead`, `SequenceHalfHeadSide`, its central connections and the participants it creates or destroys), `SequenceNote` and `SequenceHighlightColor`: a sequence diagram as its text describes it.
- `EntityRelationshipLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:limits:)` lays an entity relationship diagram out and returns an `EntityRelationshipLayout` (each entity's box with its name and attribute rows, each relationship's path, cardinality marks and label, the groups' boxes in `subgraphs`, the title), or throws `EntityRelationshipLayoutError` past the layout limit (`subgraphsNotLaidOut` is kept for callers and thrown only for groups holding each other; a group related to itself loops off its box's right side). `EntityRelationshipDrawingParts(layout:)` groups its geometry by how it is painted, and `EntityRelationshipRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it, types, keys and comments in the text color at `EntityRelationshipDrawingParts.mutedTextOpacity`.
- `EntityRelationshipDiagram`, `EntityRelationshipEntity`, `EntityRelationshipAttribute` (with `EntityRelationshipAttributeKey`), `EntityRelationship` (with `EntityRelationshipCardinality` and `EntityRelationshipIdentification`), `EntityRelationshipSubgraph`: an entity relationship diagram as its text describes it.
- `GanttChart` (with `GanttTask`): a Gantt chart as its text describes it, dates as written. `GanttLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:now:timeZone:limits:)` schedules it on the moment and in the time zone given and returns a `GanttLayout` (the title, and the plot: rows, bars and milestones with their paint, text, section bands, excluded days, grid, axis dates, today's line), or throws `GanttLayoutError`; `GanttDrawingParts(layout:)` and `GanttRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paint it, in `DiagramPalette`'s accent, critical, muted and second surface colors.
- `GitGraphDiagram` (with `GitGraphCommit`, `GitGraphBranch`, `GitGraphCommitKind`, `GitGraphCommitType` and `GitGraphDirection`): a Git graph once its statements are played, commits in the order they were made with their branch and parents, branches in lane order. `GitGraphLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` returns a `GitGraphLayout` (lanes with their lines and names, connectors with their style, commits with their symbol, turned id and tags, the title), or throws `GitGraphLayoutError` for a graph that sets options not applied; `GitGraphDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by series color, and `GitGraphRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it.
- `UserJourneyDiagram` (with `UserJourneyTask` and `UserJourneyMood`): a user journey as its text describes it. `UserJourneyLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` returns a `UserJourneyLayout` (the section bands, each task's card, text, face, stem and actors' dots, the scale's levels, the line from face to face, the legend, the title); `UserJourneyDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by how it is painted, the dots by series color; `UserJourneyRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it.
- `QuadrantChart` (with `QuadrantChartPoint`, `QuadrantChartPointStyle` and `QuadrantChartColor`): a quadrant chart as its text describes it. `QuadrantChartLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` returns a `QuadrantChartLayout` (the quadrants' boxes and texts, the axes' texts, turned or not, the points with their paint, outline and placed text, the title); `QuadrantChartDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by how it is painted, the points back to front by paint; `QuadrantChartRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it, a point in `DiagramPalette.seriesColor(at: 0)` unless the chart writes its color.
- `PieChart` (with `PieChartSlice`): a pie chart as its text describes it. `PieChartLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` returns a `PieChartLayout` (the pie's center and radius, each drawn slice's angles and percentage, the legend's swatches and labels, the title); `PieChartDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by how it is painted, one fill per series color; `PieChartRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it, the slices in `DiagramPalette.series`.
- `XYChart` (with `XYChartXAxis`, `XYChartXAxisScale`, `XYChartYAxis`, `XYChartValueRange`, `XYChartSeries` and `XYChartOrientation`): an XY chart as Mermaid's database holds it, each series' values placed along the x axis. `XYChartLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` returns an `XYChartLayout` (the plot's frame, the grid, the axes and their labels and titles, the bars, the lines' runs and dots, the point labels, the legend, the title) or throws `XYChartLayoutError.noSeries`; `XYChartDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by how it is painted, and `XYChartRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it, the series in `DiagramPalette.series`.
- `VennDiagram` (with `VennSubset`, `VennText`, `VennStyle` and `VennStyleDeclaration`): a Venn diagram as Mermaid's database holds it. `VennLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:limits:)` returns a `VennLayout` (each set's circle with its series index and `VennAppearance`, the overlaps a style outlines with their `VennRegionOutline`, each region's lines with their colors, the title) or throws `VennLayoutError.tooManyOverlaps`; `VennRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints its scene.
- `CynefinDiagram` (with `CynefinDomain`, `CynefinTransition` and `CynefinOptions`): a Cynefin framework as Mermaid's database holds it. `CynefinLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` returns a `CynefinLayout` (the regions, the boundaries and the cliff, the confusion ellipse, each domain's block with its badges, the transitions with their arrows and labels, the title); `CynefinRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints its scene.
- `WardleyMap` (with `WardleyMapNode`, `WardleyMapLink`, `WardleyMapTrend`, `WardleyMapPipeline`, `WardleyMapAnnotation`, `WardleyMapNote`, `WardleyMapForce` and `WardleyMapOptions`): a Wardley map as Mermaid's builder holds it, coordinates in percent. `WardleyMapLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:fittingWidth:)` returns a `WardleyMapLayout` (the plot, the stages' lines and names, the axes and their titles, pipelines, links, evolutions, nodes with their labels, annotations and their list, notes, forces, the title); `WardleyMapRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints its scene.
- `KanbanBoard` (with `KanbanColumn`, `KanbanCard`, `KanbanPriority` and `KanbanOptions`): a kanban board as its text describes it. `KanbanLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:maximumWidth:)` returns a `KanbanLayout` (each column's box, title lines and count, each card's box, priority mark, text, ticket and assignee lines, the title), folded into rows when wider than `maximumWidth`; `KanbanRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints its scene.
- `TreeViewDiagram` (with `TreeViewEntry`, `TreeViewOptions`, `TreeViewIconMaps` and `TreeViewGlyph`): a tree view as its text describes it. `TreeViewLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:maximumWidth:)` returns a `TreeViewLayout` (each row's band, icon, name and description lines, the guide lines, the title), tight when wider than `maximumWidth` and tight is narrower; `TreeViewRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints its scene.
- `IshikawaDiagram` (with `IshikawaNode`): an Ishikawa diagram as its text describes it. `IshikawaLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:maximumWidth:)` returns an `IshikawaLayout`: across, the spine, its arrow, each category's bones, arrow, box, name and causes, and the head; or, when wider than a `maximumWidth` under Pele's 640-point breakpoint and narrower so, down the page (`outlineEntries`, `outlineConnectors`); `IshikawaRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints its scene.
- `SankeyDiagram` (with `SankeyNode`, `SankeyLink`, `SankeyOptions` and `SankeyNodeAlignment`): a Sankey diagram as its rows and its front matter's options describe it. `SankeyLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` returns a `SankeyLayout` (each node's bar, each link's band, each node's label, the title) or throws `SankeyLayoutError` (circular links, or options not drawn yet); `SankeyDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by how it is painted, and `SankeyRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it, each band a gradient between two of `DiagramPalette.series`.
- `EventModelDiagram` (with `EventModelFrame`, `EventModelEntityKind`, `EventModelLane`, `EventModelLaneGroup`, `EventModelRelation`, `EventModelNote` and `EventModelSpecification`): an event model as Mermaid's database builds it. `EventModelLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` lays it out across and `layout(_:typography:columnWidth:)` down a column, each an `EventModelLayout` (the lanes' bands and names, each frame's box, kind, name, number and data lines, the relations' paths and arrows, the cards, the title); `EventModelDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by how it is painted, and `EventModelRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it.
- `RailroadDiagram` (with `RailroadNotation`, `RailroadRule`, `RailroadExpression` and `RailroadOptions`): a railroad diagram's rules as one tree per rule, whatever its notation. `RailroadLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:)` lays it out and `layout(_:typography:columnWidth:)` lays it out tight for a column, each a `RailroadLayout` (per rule its name, track, arrows, end dots, boxes and counts); `RailroadDrawingParts(layout:)` groups it by how it is painted, and `RailroadRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints it.

- `ArchitectureDiagram` (with `ArchitectureNode`, `ArchitectureGroup`, `ArchitectureEdge`, `ArchitectureSide`, `ArchitectureAlignment` and `ArchitectureOptions`): an architecture diagram's nodes, groups, edges and alignments. `ArchitectureLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:availableWidth:isTight:)` lays it out (`ArchitectureLayout`, with `ArchitectureLayoutGroup`, `ArchitectureLayoutEdge`, `ArchitectureLayoutNode` and `ArchitectureLayoutIcon`); `DiagramScene(layout:)` paints it and `ArchitectureRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` draws it.
- `UseCaseDiagram` (with `UseCaseActor`, `UseCase`, `UseCaseSystemBoundary`, `UseCaseRelationship`, `UseCaseNote`, `UseCaseJSONNode` and its ordered `UseCaseJSONValue`, `UseCaseClassDefinition` and `UseCaseDirection`): a use case diagram as Pele's model holds it, everything in the order first mentioned. `UseCaseLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:limits:)` returns a `UseCaseLayout` (the boundaries with their titles and tabs, the lines with their marks, dashes and labels, the nodes with their forms, texts, rows and `UseCaseLook`), and `layout(_:typography:limits:fittingWidth:)` turns it down as Pele's `turnToFit` does; `UseCaseRenderer.draw(_:in:palette:)` paints its scene. - `InfoDiagram`, `PacketDiagram` (with `PacketField` and `PacketOptions`), `RadarChart` (with `RadarAxis`, `RadarCurve`, `RadarGraticule`, `RadarChartOptions` and `RadarAppearance`) and `TreemapDiagram` (with `TreemapNode`, `TreemapOptions` and `TreemapClassTextStyle`): the four simple charts as their texts describe them. `InfoDiagramLayoutEngine`, `PacketDiagramLayoutEngine`, `RadarChartLayoutEngine` and `TreemapLayoutEngine` (each `layout(_:typography:)`, none refusing anything) return an `InfoDiagramLayout`, `PacketDiagramLayout` (a box per field and row with its fitted label, the bit numbers, the title), `RadarChartLayout` (rings, spokes, curves with their points, labels, legend, title) or `TreemapLayout` (a box per node with its fitted name and value, its class's style and its corner radius, the title); `PacketDiagramDrawingParts`, `RadarChartDrawingParts` and `TreemapDrawingParts` group them by how they are painted, and `InfoDiagramRenderer`, `PacketDiagramRenderer`, `RadarChartRenderer` and `TreemapRenderer` paint their scenes. `MermaidDiagramsVersion` is the name and version the info diagram prints.

## Adding a diagram type

A new type needs four things, and nothing in a reader: its parser (a case of `MermaidDiagram`, read by `MermaidDiagramReader`), its layout engine and layout, its drawing parts and its scene (`DiagramScene(layout:)`, back to front, every color a `DiagramPaint`), and one case in `MermaidDiagramPreparationContext.prepared(_:)`, which lays it out, makes its scene, its narrow scene if it turns to run down, and what a screen reader says of it (`MermaidDiagramAccessibility`), and turns its layout's errors into a `MermaidDiagramPreparationFailure` case. A case of `MermaidDiagramKind` gives Pele's name for its SVG. Run `scripts/pele/run.sh` again for its documentation examples to be drawn by Pele, add a test to `PeleDifferentialTests` for its corpus and list in `PeleKnownDifferences.md` what differs. `DiagramSceneRenderer`, `DiagramSVGWriter` and every reader that paints scenes then draw it as they draw the others. Add its documentation examples to the benchmark's corpora and to `DiagramSceneSamples` so the scene, SVG and preparation tests cover it.

## Narrow columns

`FlowchartDiagram`, `StateDiagram`, `ClassDiagram`, `EntityRelationshipDiagram`, `GitGraphDiagram`, `TimelineDiagram`, `RequirementDiagram` and `UseCaseDiagram` each give `turnedToRunDown`: the diagram with what runs across turned to run down, or nil when nothing does, which a reader lays out again for a column too narrow for the diagram, as Pele does. `FlowchartLayoutEngine.layout(_:typography:limits:spacing:)` takes `DiagramSpacing.compact` for Pele's tight gaps (half the nodes' separation, three quarters of the ranks', 12 points of group padding), and `layout(_:typography:limits:availableWidth:)` fits a container as Pele does: its own direction turned from the top down (`rootTurnedToRunDown`, its groups keeping theirs) under the breakpoint, then tight when that is narrower; `ClassDiagramLayoutEngine`, `StateDiagramLayoutEngine`, `SequenceLayoutEngine`, `SwimlaneLayoutEngine`, `TimelineLayoutEngine`, `PacketDiagramLayoutEngine`, `PieChartLayoutEngine`, `RadarChartLayoutEngine` and `TreemapLayoutEngine` have the same `availableWidth` layout. A class diagram wider than its container has its parts that nothing joins set in rows that fit it (`ClassDiagramComponentRows`, Pele's `wrapComponents`). A requirement diagram turned runs from the top down whichever way it ran across, as Pele turns it. A swimlane diagram is laid out again turned and with Pele's tight measurements (half the gaps across, three quarters along, text wrapped at 100 points, 12 points of padding, titles wrapped to the column). A kanban board, a tree view and an Ishikawa diagram are laid out again for the narrowest column with that width as `maximumWidth`: the board folded into rows of columns, the tree tight, the fishbone down the page. A C4 diagram is drawn tight for it, as Pele's `tighten` draws it, with the gap between columns that leaves the fewest relations without a lane of their own. A block diagram and an architecture diagram are laid out again tight for the narrowest column, as Pele's `tighten` draws it. An agent flow that runs across is laid out again to run down, as Pele's `turnToFit` does.

## Limits

`MermaidDiagramLimits.standard` bounds the text to 50,000 UTF-8 bytes (Mermaid's default), a diagram to 5,000 nodes (a state diagram's states and notes together) and 5,000 edges (Pele's edge limit; `A & B --> C & D` makes four edges, so a short text can ask for many; a pie chart's slices, a Gantt chart's tasks, a Git graph's commits, branches and tags, a user journey's sections and tasks and a quadrant chart's points and classes count as nodes, and so do an XY chart's categories and values together; the actors named on a user journey's tasks count as edges, and a Sankey diagram's nodes and links as nodes and edges; a Venn diagram's sets, overlaps and texts count as nodes and its unions' members and styles as edges, and the pairwise overlaps it states and implies, and the circles its regions weigh while they look for room, are bounded by the layout points; a Cynefin framework's items count as nodes and its transitions as edges, and a Wardley map's anchors, components, notes, annotations and forces as nodes and its links as edges, and a C4 diagram's shapes and boundaries as nodes and its relations as edges, and a use case diagram's actors, use cases, boundaries, notes and JSON tables as nodes (each declaring statement before they are resolved, each distinct one after) and its relationships and notes' connectors as edges, its JSON read on a stack of its own however deep it nests; an event model's frames, notes and specifications count as nodes and its relations as edges, and a railroad diagram's rules and their parts as nodes, each rule's tree and brackets nested at most 64 levels deep, `RailroadExpression.maximumNestingDepth`; a requirement diagram's requirements and elements count as nodes and its relations as edges, and the style declarations its `style`, `class`, `classDef` and `:::` copy onto boxes, with the classes given, are at most Pele's million, `RequirementDiagramBuilder.maximumStyleCopyCount`; a swimlane diagram is bounded as a flowchart is, and its lane layout passes through at most 20,000 points and bounds its routing's work as Pele's does), the layout to 50,000 points, a Gantt chart's scheduling to 500,000 days stepped through, and a drawing to 20,000 points on a side (`allowsDrawing`: a reader shows a larger layout's source instead). `MermaidDiagram.hasNothingToDraw` tells a diagram that declares nothing drawn. A style is applied only for the properties a look has room for, within bounds (a line at most 16 points wide, dash patterns of at most 16 lengths, together at least 2 points). Each count is checked before anything of that count is made, and a diagram past a limit fails with a typed error. A packet diagram's fields and its rows (bits over `bitsPerRow`), a radar chart's axes and curves together, a treemap's rows and its `classDef` statements count as nodes, a radar chart's values as edges (past them `tooManyChartValues`); a packet diagram's rows cannot be asked for in the billions, and a number past 2^53 is read as 2^53.
A block diagram's blocks and its space cells (`space:N` is N of them) count as nodes and its links as edges, counted while read; composites nest without recursion, read, set into their hierarchy and laid out breadth first. An architecture diagram's groups, services and junctions count as nodes and its edges and alignments' members as edges, checked before any is made.
