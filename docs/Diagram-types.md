# Diagram types

What the package reads and draws of each of Mermaid's types, beside Pele's drawing of it. The [gallery](Gallery.md) shows one example of each.

| Type | State |
| --- | --- |
| Flowchart (`flowchart`, `graph`) | Drawn. Laid out in ranks, subgraphs as nested boxes in their own directions. Read: the direction, nodes in every shape of Pele's catalogue (the classic ones by bracket or `@{ shape: … }`, the ellipse `(-text-)`, and the 38 named only, from `cloud` and `person` to `docs` and `fork`; a name Mermaid does not know, or written with a capital, is refused as Mermaid refuses it), quoted and Markdown text, entity codes, icon and picture nodes (`@{ icon: …, form: …, pos: …, h: … }`, `@{ img: … }`, laid out and framed as Pele lays them out; the icon's glyph and the picture are not drawn, as Pele draws them only through its host), edges with their strokes, marks, lengths and labels, chains and `&`, groups nested as Mermaid nests them, `accTitle` and `accDescr`. Styles and `click` are counted and not applied; a collapsed subgraph (`@{ view: collapsed }`) is drawn as one node in Pele's collapsed-group shape, its edges meeting it. |
| State diagram (`stateDiagram`, `stateDiagram-v2`) | Drawn, composite states as boxes around their states, their concurrent regions side by side between dashed lines. As in Pele, a transition between a composite and a state inside it runs from just inside the composite's border, notes beside a composite are laid out beside its box, and its loops hang off the box. Read: states with their texts (`Id : text`, `state "text" as Id`), transitions with their text, `[*]` starts and ends, `<<choice>>`, `<<fork>>` and `<<join>>`, notes (`note left of`, `note right of`, on one line or up to `end note`), composite bodies with their regions (`--`), `direction`, `accTitle` and `accDescr`. Styles (`classDef`, `class`, `:::`, `style`) and `click` are counted and not applied; floating notes are read and not drawn, as in Mermaid. |
| Class diagram (`classDiagram`, `classDiagram-v2`) | Drawn. Laid out in ranks, namespaces as nested boxes as Pele draws them (a dotted name inside its prefixes, a note in the namespace it is written in, the front matter's `hierarchicalNamespaces: false` read; each part of a namespace's name counts as a node of the limits). Read: classes with labels, generic types, annotations and members (visibility, static, abstract, return types), every relation mark on either side with cardinalities and text, lollipop interfaces, notes, the direction, `accTitle` and `accDescr`. Styles, `click`, `link` and `callback` are counted and not applied. |
| Sequence diagram (`sequenceDiagram`) | Drawn, in Pele's measurements. Read: participants and actors with aliases, participant attributes (`@{ "type": …, "alias": … }`, read as YAML) and Pele's boundary, control, entity, database, collections and queue shapes, `box … end` with its color and title, `create` and `destroy`, every arrow (`->`, `-->`, `->>`, `-->>`, `-x`, `--x`, `-)`, `--)`, `<<->>`, `<<-->>`, the half arrows `-\|\`, `-\|/`, `-\\`, `-//`, their dotted forms and their reverses), central connections (`()`), activations, notes, `loop`, `alt`, `opt`, `par`, `critical`, `break` and `rect` blocks, `autonumber`, `title`, `accTitle` and `accDescr`. Participant menus (`link`, `links`, `properties`, `details`) are read and dropped, as Pele draws nothing for them. For a narrow column it is drawn with Pele's tight measurements (`SequenceMeasurements.tight`: 8 points between participants, every text wrapped at each participant's share of the column and broken between characters past 120 points), the room the column still has given to the gaps wrapped messages cross (`SequenceGapWidening`). |
| Entity relationship (`erDiagram`) | Drawn. Laid out in ranks, `subgraph … end` groups as nested boxes in their own directions, as Pele draws them (a name that is a group's identifier stands for its box, a group related to itself loops off its box, a title is Markdown). Read: entities by bare, quoted or numeric name, aliases, attribute blocks (type with `?`, name, `PK`, `FK` and `UK` keys, comment; backticks and `~` generics), relationships with every cardinality in symbols or words, identifying and non-identifying lines and labels, `direction`, `accTitle` and `accDescr`. Styles and classes are counted and not applied. |
| Mindmap (`mindmap`) | Drawn, Pele's way: the root in the middle, branches to its right and left in the series colors. Read: the tree from indentation, every shape (`[ ]`, `( )`, `(( ))`, `) (`, `)) ((`, `{{ }}`, none), quoted and Markdown text, `%%` comments, `accTitle` and `accDescr` (not in Mermaid's mindmap grammar). Icons and classes are counted and not drawn. In a column too narrow for the map (under 640 points) it runs down as an outline, each level set in, as Pele turns it. |
| Timeline (`timeline`) | Drawn, Pele's way: across, or down for `timeline TD`, periods and sections in the series colors. Read: `title`, `section`, periods with their events on their line and the lines after, `accTitle` and `accDescr`, `%%` and `#` comments; only `<br>` is markup in the text. Front matter configuration (`disableMulticolor`) is not read. |
| Gantt chart (`gantt`) | Drawn: a row per task (bars, milestones), section bands and names, excluded days, today's line, the axis' dates. Read and scheduled as Mermaid does: `dateFormat`, `axisFormat`, `tickInterval`, `weekday`, `inclusiveEndDates`, `topAxis`, `excludes`, `includes`, `weekend`, `todayMarker`, `title`, `accTitle`, `accDescr`, sections, tasks with `done`, `active`, `crit`, `milestone`, identifiers, `after`, `until`, dates and durations. `click` is read and marks its tasks and sets their links, as in Pele, which runs no call; a task tagged `vert` is a dashed line across the rows with its text under the axis, on as many lines as keep the texts apart. Dates that `dateFormat` does not read are read as JavaScript's `Date` reads them (ISO 8601 and V8's legacy forms: `7 March 2014`, `3/7/2014`, `Fri, 07 Mar 2014 10:00:00 GMT`; a comma would end the task's field). The front matter's and directives' `gantt` options are applied (`GanttOptions`: `fontSize`, `sectionFontSize`, `barHeight`, `barGap`, `numberSectionStyles`, `leftPadding`, `rightPadding`, `useWidth`, `topPadding`, `gridLineStartPadding`, `topAxis`, `axisFormat`, `tickInterval`, `weekday`), `todayMarker` with declarations restyles today's line (`GanttTodayLineStyle`), and `displayMode: compact` (under `gantt:` or the front matter's own key) packs each section's tasks, sorted by start, on the first row free when they start, as Pele does. The page narrows to a narrow column, not under 280 points. |
| Pie chart (`pie`) | Drawn: slices clockwise from the top in the series colors, percentages inside or outside the rim, the legend on the right, the title above. Read: `showData`, `title`, `accTitle`, `accDescr`, slices (`"label" : value`). The front matter's and directives' `textPosition`, `donutHole` and `legendPosition` are applied (`PieChartOptions`); `highlightSlice` is read and draws nothing, as Pele's pie renderer does not read it. For a narrow column the legend beside the pie moves below it and the pie shrinks by what the page overflows, not under 140 points across, as Pele's `fitWidth` draws it. |
| Git graph (`gitGraph`) | Drawn, Pele's way: a lane per branch in the series colors, commits as dots, squares, crossed dots, rings and cherries, ids turned an eighth of a turn, tags, branch names, Pele's curves between lanes; across, or down (`TB:`) or up (`BT:`). Read and played as Mermaid's database plays them: `commit` with `id:`, `msg:`, `tag:` and `type:`, `branch` with `order:`, `checkout` and `switch`, `merge` with `id:`, `tag:` and `type:`, `cherry-pick` with `id:`, `tag:` and `parent:`, `title`, `accTitle`, `accDescr`; what Mermaid refuses is a typed `GitGraphSyntaxProblem`, a repeated commit id included. The `gitGraph` options of the front matter (block, or a flow mapping, or inside `config: {…}`) and of directives are applied (`GitGraphOptions`: `mainBranchName`, `mainBranchOrder`, `showBranches`, `showCommitLabel`, `rotateCommitLabel`, `parallelCommits`); any other option and a value of the wrong kind set nothing, as Pele reads six and no more, and no graph is refused for its options (`GitGraphLayoutError.optionsNotApplied` is never thrown now). |
| User journey (`journey`) | Drawn, Pele's way across the page: cards under their sections' bands, a scale from 5 to 1 with a happy, neutral or sad face per task at its score and a line from face to face, the actors' dots in the series colors and their legend. Read: `title`, `section`, tasks (`Task: score: actor, actor`, the score read as JavaScript's `Number` reads it), `accTitle`, `accDescr`, `%%` and `#` comments; only `<br>` is markup in the text. In a column under 640 points that its cards side by side do not fit, it is a column of cards (`UserJourneyLayoutEngine.columnLayout`, `UserJourneyColumnLayout`): each with its text, its face and score and a row per actor, as Pele turns it. |
| Quadrant chart (`quadrantChart`) | Drawn, Pele's way on a page of 500 points: the four quadrants with their texts, the axes' texts (the y axis's turned), the points at their coordinates in their styles and their classes', each text at the first free place around its point, the title. Read: `title`, `x-axis` and `y-axis` with their low and high texts, `quadrant-1` to `quadrant-4`, points (`Name:::class: [x, y] radius: 10, color: #ff3300, stroke-color: #10f0f0, stroke-width: 5px`), `classDef`, `accTitle`, `accDescr`, `%%` comments. The front matter's and directives' `quadrantChart` options are applied (`QuadrantChartOptions`: `chartWidth`, `chartHeight`, the five font sizes, `quadrantPadding`, the label and title paddings, `quadrantTextTopPadding`, `pointRadius`, `pointTextPadding`, `xAxisPosition`, `yAxisPosition`); its theme variables are not, as Pele does not apply them (see Theme palettes). The page narrows to a narrow column, not under 280 points, its height with it. |
| XY chart (`xychart`, `xychart-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way: bars and lines in the series colors over a grid, the axes with their marks and titles, the legend on the right; `horizontal` turns the axes. Read as Mermaid's grammar reads it: `vertical` or `horizontal`, `title`, `x-axis` (a title, then categories in brackets or a range `a --> b`), `y-axis` (a title, then a range), `bar` and `line` with their values and a line's point labels, `accTitle`, `accDescr`, `%%` comments. The front matter's and directives' `xyChart` options are applied (`XYChartOptions`: `width`, `height`, `chartOrientation`, `showDataLabel`, `showTitle`, `showLegend`, and for each axis `showLabel`, `showTitle`, `showTick`, `showAxisLine`); of the theme variables only `xyChart.plotColorPalette` is (see Theme palettes). The page narrows to a narrow column, not under 240 points, its height by at least 0.6 of the width's share. |
| Sankey diagram (`sankey`, `sankey-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way: nodes as bars in columns in the series colors, bands between them in a gradient from their source's color to their target's, each node's name and value beside its bar. Read as Mermaid's grammar reads it: rows of `source,target,value`, plain or quoted (commas, line breaks and doubled quotation marks inside quotation marks), `%%` comment lines, values as `parseFloat` reads them. The front matter's `config` options `width`, `height`, `nodeWidth`, `nodePadding`, `showValues`, `prefix`, `suffix` and `nodeAlignment` are applied; `labelStyle: outlined` (labels outlined in the page's color), `linkColor` (`gradient`, `source`, `target` or a color) and `nodeColors` (by node name) are drawn; a diagram whose links run in a circle is not laid out. The chart narrows to a narrow column, not under 160 points, its labels shrinking with it. |
| Event model (`eventmodeling`) | Drawn, Pele's way: across, a band per lane (the user interfaces and automations, the commands and read models, the events, and one more per namespace), each frame staggered under the end of the one before so that its arrow drops straight, relations straight or in an S between lanes, boxes in each kind's series color, the data in the monospaced font, the notes' and given, when and then specifications' cards below; down a narrow column, a card per frame under its lane's name, relations that skip a card on rails beside the cards. Read as Mermaid's grammar and database read it: `tf`, `timeframe`, `rf` and `resetframe` frames with their number, kind and qualified name, `->>` sources, `[[data]]` references and data in braces or quotes with its type in backticks, `entity`, `data` blocks, `note` and `gwt` statements, `title`, `accTitle`, `accDescr`, `%%`, `//` and `/* */` comments; a frame number used twice is refused, as in Mermaid. Mermaid's rules of which kind may feed which are stated (`EventModelEntityKind.acceptsInput(from:)`) and, as in Mermaid, not applied. |
| Railroad diagram (`railroad-beta`, `railroad-ebnf-beta`, `railroad-abnf-beta`, `railroad-peg-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way: a track per rule under its name from dot to dot, terminals in pills in the monospaced font, names of rules in boxes, special sequences in dashed boxes in italics, choices stacked below the baseline, options bypassed above, repetitions looping below with their counts (`2×`, `≥2×`, `≤4×`, `2–4×`); for a narrow column, Pele's tighter gaps with long sequences folded into rows. Read in each of Mermaid's four notations into one expression tree: its constructor calls, EBNF in the W3C and ISO styles (exceptions, postfixes, `(* *)` and `/* */` comments), ABNF as in RFC 5234 (repeats, numeric values, `;` comments) and PEG (predicates shown as special text), with `title`, `accTitle` and `accDescr`; the keywords are detected without regard to case, as Mermaid detects them, and refused unless written in lower case, as its grammars refuse them. The front matter's `config: railroad:` options are applied (`fontSize`, `padding`, `horizontalSeparation`, `verticalSeparation`, `arcRadius`, `showMarkers`, `markerRadius`, `strokeWidth`). |
| Block diagram (`block`, `block-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way: blocks in rows of `columns N` (one row without), every column as wide as the widest block, every row with a plain block as tall as the tallest, spans (`id:3`) stopping at the last column, `space` and `space:N` cells, composites (`block … end`, `block:id["Title"]:2 … end`) nested to any depth with their own columns and a title band, the fourteen bracket shapes of a flowchart (circles and diamonds at their own size, the slanted shapes only widened), block arrows (`id<["text"]>(right)`, `x`, `y`, and boxes with a point per direction); edges straight between blocks sharing a row or a column, else one rounded bend or straight across, whichever crosses the fewest blocks, edges between the same blocks spread apart, their texts stacked along their line and the gap between two blocks widened where the texts do not fit, loops as arcs round a block's top right corner; `classDef`, `class` and `style` (fill, stroke, its width and dashes, text color, bold, italic, size), with Pele's dark or white text on a fill a style sets; the front matter's `block.padding`; for a narrow column, Pele's tight drawing (6-point gaps, text wrapped at 80 points). Read with Pele's lexer and parser, ported state for state: every link of `A --> B --> C` kept, blocks merged by identifier, classes and styles waiting for a block declared later, comment lines taken out as Pele's preprocessing takes them out (so a quoted text running over one keeps no line of it). An edge that crosses the border of a composite a style fills runs over a casing of the page's color, and one that a filled composite holds is drawn in that fill's dark or white ink, as Pele draws them. |
| Architecture diagram (`architecture-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way, on a grid rather than Mermaid's force solver: every service and junction in a cell, an edge's sides putting its ends beside each other (facing sides in line, perpendicular ones diagonally, like sides stacked across), taken cells slid past, alignments (`align row`, `align column`) followed ahead of edges, each group a solid rectangle of cells laid out innermost first and placed in its parent as one block, unconnected parts side by side in rows that keep the whole roughly square; empty rows and columns take no room, an edge's text widens the gap it crosses, groups keep their padding and title band on the rows and columns at their edges; edges orthogonal from side to side with rounded corners, from a group's border for `{group}` (carried on to a junction inside), arrows for `<` and `>`, their texts on their longest run moved off the boxes beside it, a group's title after the edges coming down through its corner; services as 48-point squares with Pele's built-in icons (cloud, database, disk, internet, server) or their text cut with an ellipsis, junctions as dots; the options `iconSize`, `fontSize` and `padding`; for a narrow column, Pele's tight drawing. Read with Pele's Langium terminals (an identifier starting with L, R, T or B reads as a side, as in Mermaid) and checked as its database checks them. Not drawn: icons of an icon pack (`logos:aws-ec2`), whose slot stays empty as Pele leaves it without a resolver. |
| Agent flow (`agentflow-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way, on the flowchart's compound layout: nodes in the shapes an agent flow keeps (`task` a rounded box, `tool` a subroutine, `input` leaning right, `decision` a diamond, `refdoc` a lined document, `action` a hexagon, a connector a rounded box), each washed in its kind's series color and outlined in it, text wrapped at 120 points (lines of a text of several 1.3 sizes apart) and every shape but a diamond at least 120 wide, the edges on one side of a node meeting in its middle; `flow … end` containers nested as boxes washed in the slots above the kinds, `@{ view: collapsed }` drawing a flow as one node with a dashed line and three dots that edges inside it meet; `global … end` keeping nodes out of flows; `-->` a step, `--x` a failure, `-.-` a dotted reference, edge text with `-- text -->` or `|text|`, `&` groups; an edge between a node and a flow around it straight to the flow's border; directions inside flows, taken away from a flow an edge crosses as Mermaid does; `classDef`, `class`, `:::`, `style` and `linkStyle` (fill, stroke, its width and dashes, text color, with Pele's dark or white text on a fill a style sets, and a node's font size, weight and slant); an edge that a filled flow holds drawn in that fill's dark or white ink, one that crosses its border over a casing of the page's color; the `agentflow:` options `wrappingWidth`, `minNodeWidth`, `nodeSpacing` and `rankSpacing`; for a narrow column, a diagram that runs across laid out again to run down. Read as Pele's port of Mermaid's agentflow lexer, grammar and database reads it, metadata (`@{ … }`) as YAML on one line or several, with Mermaid's diagnostics (a removed or unsupported shape, a containment loop dropped) and each statement's place in the text kept on the model. Links, clicks, tooltips, curves and the font of a flow's title are read and not drawn. |
| Venn diagram (`venn-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way: a circle per set, its area in proportion to its size, each pair as far apart as its overlap asks (found by bisection; an overlap of three or more sets implies the pairs it holds), two and three sets exactly and more by relaxing a ring; the largest circle 100 points in radius, grown up to 1.6 times until each region holds its text at its roomiest point (a numeric search), a text still too wide wrapped to its region; the circles in the series colors at a fifth of their opacity, an overlap outlined only when a style gives it a look. Read as Mermaid's Jison lexer and grammar read it: `title`, `set` with a label in brackets and a size after a colon, `union` of two or more declared sets, `text` in a region or indented under a set or union, `style` with its declarations (`fill`, `fill-opacity`, `stroke`, `stroke-width`, `stroke-dasharray`, `stroke-opacity`, `opacity` and `color` are drawn, colors as hexadecimal, names, `rgb(…)` or, quoted, `hsl(…)`, and text over a fill is dark or light by the fill's luminance, as Pele's `ink` picks it; `font-weight` bold and `font-style` italic draw a region's text so, a set's or overlap's label measured so too; a value Pele leaves out, with `;`, braces or `url(`, is left out; other properties, `font-size` among them, are counted and not drawn), `%%` comments. |
| Cynefin framework (`cynefin-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way: four quadrants (Complex, Complicated, Chaotic, Clear) in the series colors around the confusion ellipse, Mermaid's seeded wavy boundaries between them (one step of mulberry32 per point, from the configured seed or the hash of Pele's identifier prefix, number for number) and the S-shaped cliff between Chaotic and Clear; each domain's name, decision model and practice, and its items in rounded boxes (at most three in confusion, then `+N more`), the quadrants grown to hold their blocks clear of the ellipse; transitions as curves bowing away from the middle, cut at their blocks, with their labels. Read as Mermaid's Langium grammar reads it: `title`, `accTitle`, `accDescr`, domain blocks of quoted items (a later block of a domain replaces an earlier one), transitions `a --> b : "label"` (to itself left out), `%%` comments and directives. The front matter's `cynefin` options `width`, `height`, `showDomainDescriptions`, `boundaryAmplitude` and `seed` are applied. |
| Wardley map (`wardley-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way at its natural width: the plot from the map's `size` (else the options', else 900 by 600) less Mermaid's 48-point margins, the stages' dashed lines and names (Mermaid's four, or the map's with their `@` widths), the axes and their titles; anchors as bold names, components as circles with their labels up and right unless a link leaves that way, sourcing as rings (`build`, `buy`, `outsource`, `market` with its triangle of dots), inertia as a bar, pipelines as a box with their parent as a square on top, links stopped at their nodes with arrows by their flow and labels, evolutions dashed to open circles in the accent color, annotations numbered with their list in a box (under the map without a place), bold notes, and accelerators and deaccelerators as wide arrows. Read as Mermaid's Langium grammar reads it and filled as Mermaid's builder fills it: `title`, `accTitle`, `accDescr`, `size`, `evolution`, `anchor`, `component` with `label [x, y]`, sourcing and inertia, links with ports (`+>`, `+<`, `+<>`), arrows (`->`, `-->`, `-.->`, `+'label'>`) and `; label`, `evolve`, `pipeline` blocks, `note`, `annotations`, `annotation`, `accelerator`, `deaccelerator`; coordinates up to 1 are fractions, up to 100 percentages, any other refused (`WardleyMapSyntaxProblem.coordinateOutOfRange`). The front matter's `wardley-beta` options `width`, `height` and `showGrid` are applied; for a narrow column the map is drawn again as Pele's `fitWidth` draws it: tight, its labels wrapped narrower, its plot narrowed by what is still too wide (at least 160 points), its height falling with it to three quarters. |
| C4 diagram (`C4Context`, `C4Container`, `C4Component`, `C4Dynamic`, `C4Deployment`) | Drawn, Pele's way on Mermaid's grid: in each boundary its shapes in rows of `c4ShapeInRow` (4) stretched to their row's height, then the boundaries inside it in rows of `c4BoundaryInRow` (2), each sized around what it holds; people with their head, databases as cylinders, queues as cylinders on their side, from their kind or a `$shape`, `$sprite` or tag; external elements hollow and dashed, deployment nodes solid, other boundaries dashed with their name, `[type]` and description; each shape's `«kind»`, name in bold, `[technology]` and description. Relations as Pele's route.ts routes them: straight between outlines through a shared column or row, two opposite ones 14 points apart with their labels either side, a loop for an element related to itself, and around the shapes in the way through the gaps between columns or rows or along the outside of a column, each a lane apart from the routes before it; labels slid along their lines to the place that covers least, headings moved to where no line crosses, relations numbered in `C4Dynamic`, and `UpdateElementStyle` and `UpdateRelStyle` colors and label offsets applied, with the text color that reads on a fill and lines inked or cased over a filled boundary. Read as Mermaid's Jison grammar reads it (Pele's lexer and parser): every macro with its arguments in their places or `$named`, an alias written again merged, boundaries in braces, `RelIndex`, `UpdateLayoutConfig`, `title`, `accTitle`, `accDescr`, `accDescription`, `%%` comments. Down a narrow column it is drawn tight: narrower shapes, as many to a row as fit, labels on their lines. Sprites, tags other than forms, links and legends are kept, not drawn. |
| Use case diagram (`usecase-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way: actors as stick figures (or hollow, filled, or a box for an icon, a slash across a business actor's head), use cases as ovals (a slash across a business one) or boxes, notes with a folded corner on a dotted connector, JSON tables of one row per leaf in the order the keys are written; laid out in ranks along `direction` (left to right unless written; `usecase.nodeSpacing` and `rankSpacing` from the options), each `systemBoundary` a box of the layered cluster layout with its title clear of the lines entering its top, or in a tab for `type: package`; `-->`, `<--`, `--`, `--o`, `o--`, `--x`, `x--` with their marks and labels, `..> : include`/`extend` dashed with «include», `--|>` with a hollow triangle, longer operators asking for more ranks, a relationship to itself as a loop; `classDef`, `class`, `style` and `:::`. Mermaid's lexer, grammar and model checks are ported from Pele's port of them, every contradiction a typed `UseCaseContradiction`; the text is read as Pele's parse hands it over (comment lines taken out, a line break added at the end), errors naming their line as written. Turned to run down for a narrow column. Not drawn: icons inside an icon actor's box (Pele draws them only with an icon resolver), Pele's straight lead into a mark at the end of a curved line, and animation (an animated relationship keeps Pele's still dashes) |
| Info diagram (`info`) | Drawn, Pele's way: the title in bold over a line of the package's name and version (`MermaidDiagramsVersion`), where Pele prints its own. Read: `showInfo`, `title`, `accTitle` and `accDescr`, and nothing else. |
| Packet diagram (`packet`, `packet-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way on a grid of bits: rows of `bitsPerRow` bits (32) with a box per field, a field that crosses a row's end split into a box per row, the first and last bit above each box, labels shrunk then cut to their box, the title under the rows. Read: `0-15: "label"`, `106: "label"`, `+16: "label"`, contiguity and the other checks of Mermaid's database as typed errors with their line, `bitsPerRow`, `bitWidth`, `rowHeight`, `paddingX`, `paddingY`, `showBits` and `bitOrder: descending`. For a column under 640 points that the rows do not fit, it turns as Pele does: fewer bits to a row (a power of two, a byte at least, `PacketTurnedGrid`), each bit as wide as the column gives it, each label wrapped into its box keeping every character, each row as high as its tallest label. |
| Radar chart (`radar-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way: axes from the top clockwise, a graticule of circles or polygons (`ticks`, at most 32), each curve a closed Catmull-Rom curve or polygon through its values between `min` and `max`, filled at a fifth and outlined in its series color, axis labels hanging off their spokes, the legend beside the chart (`showLegend`). Read: `axis`, `curve` with numbers or named entries in any order (matched with the axes once the text is read), the options, `width`, `height`, the margins, `axisScaleFactor`, `axisLabelFactor` and `curveTension`. A curve without a value for every axis is kept and left out of the drawing, as in Mermaid. For a narrow column the legend moves below the chart and the circle shrinks by what the page overflows, not under 160 points, as Pele's `fitWidth` draws it. |
| Treemap (`treemap`, `treemap-beta`) | Drawn, Pele's way: ten nodes across and down (`nodeWidth`, `nodeHeight`), squarified by value, sections as tints of their branch's series color with a header band holding their name and total (a short band or none where they are small), leaves with their name and value, gaps and insets that shrink with the average leaf's area, names shrunk then cut. Read: the outline by indentation, `"Name": value`, `:::class` with `classDef` (fill, stroke, stroke width, dashes, text color, and a font size in points that the text is cut at rather than shrunk, bold and italic applied; the rest, such as an opacity, counted in `unappliedStatementCount`), `valueFormat` (a d3-format subset: `$`, `,`, precision, `f`, `%`, `d`, `e`, `~`), `showValues`, `padding` and `diagramPadding`. For a narrow column the treemap is as wide as the column, not under 200 points (Pele's `capWidth`). |

## Theme palettes

A diagram's own theme variables (the front matter's `config.themeVariables`, or a `%%{init: …}%%` directive's `themeVariables`) set the colors of its series as Pele's `src/svg/palette.ts` reads them, for the types Pele reads them for: `pie1` to `pie8` for a pie chart, `git0` to `git7` for a Git graph, `cScale0` to `cScale7` for a timeline, a radar chart and a treemap, `cScale1` to `cScale8` for a mindmap (`cScale0` is its root's in Mermaid), `venn1` to `venn8` for a Venn diagram, those set taken again in turn, and `xyChart.plotColorPalette`, a comma list (commas inside `rgb()` and other functions kept), for an XY chart, taken again in turn. Eight slots, as Pele's `--pele-series-1` to `--pele-series-8`. Each value is checked as Pele's `safeColor` checks it: up to 200 characters of letters, digits, `_`, whitespace and `#%.,+-/()`; without parentheses a hexadecimal color, a CSS color name, `transparent` or `currentColor`; with them only CSS's color functions (`rgb`, `rgba`, `hsl`, `hsla`, `hwb`, `lab`, `lch`, `oklab`, `oklch`, `color`, `color-mix`, `light-dark`) and `var()` of Pele's own `--pele-…` variables. A value that fails keeps the theme's color for its slot.

The scene carries them (`DiagramScene.seriesColors`, a `DiagramSeriesColors` of eight optional `DiagramThemeColor`s and the label ink on each). `DiagramSceneRenderer` paints the scene's series in them over the host's palette (`DiagramScene.palette(over:)`), resolving the CSS as a browser resolves Pele's variables over a page: `var(--pele-accent)` and the other roles as the palette's, `var(--pele-series-N)` as that slot, `currentColor` as the foreground, `light-dark()` by whether the palette's background is darker than its foreground, the color functions and `color-mix()` as CSS Color 4 and 5 convert and mix (colors past sRGB are kept in extended sRGB). A slot that refers to itself through others, or to a variable no palette sets without a fallback, is no color, as in a browser, and takes Pele's own series color; a value this package does not resolve (a `color()` space other than `srgb`, `srgb-linear`, `display-p3` and the `xyz` ones, a `var()` inside a color's channels, Unicode spaces CSS does not read as spaces, functions nested deeper than 12) keeps the palette's color, where a browser may paint something else. `DiagramSVGWriter` writes them as Pele does, `;--pele-series-N:<value>` at the end of the root's style, with Pele's variables, and writes the series in them with a palette. Text on a series color takes Pele's label ink (`#1f1f1f` on a light color, white on a dark one) where Pele knows the color's contrast (a hexadecimal color, a name, `rgb()` or `hsl()` in Pele's own reading, at half opacity or more): a pie chart's percentages, a Git graph's branch names, a timeline's sections and periods, a treemap's names and values (unless the box's class sets a fill of its own: Pele then writes the ink of that fill, or none where it does not know that fill's contrast, as for `fill:none`). Pele's other entry, the host's `config` option, has no counterpart here.

Mermaid's other theme variables are read by no type, as Pele reads none of them: `primaryColor` and the other base colors, `fillType0` …, `pieOuterStrokeWidth`, `pieTitleTextSize` and the pie's other variables, `gitBranchLabel0` …, `gitInv0` …, `commitLabelColor`, `commitLabelBackground`, `commitLabelFontSize`, `tagLabelColor`, `tagLabelBackground`, `tagLabelBorder`, `tagLabelFontSize`, `cScaleLabel0` …, `cScaleInv0` …, `quadrant1Fill`, `quadrant1TextFill` and the quadrant chart's others, `xyChart.titleColor`, `xyChart.backgroundColor` and the XY chart's others but `plotColorPalette`, `radar.curveOpacity` and the radar's others, `packet.startByteColor` and the packet's others, `treeView.labelColor` and the tree view's others. They are not applied because Pele does not apply them.
