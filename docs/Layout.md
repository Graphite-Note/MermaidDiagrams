# Layout

A layered layout (Sugiyama's method) on plain values, ported step for step from Pele's `layout/layered.ts` (`LayeredFlatLayout`): the edges that close cycles are turned around by a depth-first walk in declaration order, nodes ranked by their longest paths and moved in up to eight passes toward the side with more edges, every edge cut into a bend per rank it passes (past 20,000 bends the longest drawn direct), an edge's text in a rank of its own between the ranks of nodes; each rank first ordered depth first from the top, then by keys, pins and port hints, then by barycenter sweeps that stop after four without improvement, adjacent swaps and, for graphs of up to 600 points, a second run going up first and the mirror image, the order keeping more of the written order winning a tie; positions across from Brandes and Köpf's four alignments, balanced; the ends of edges on one side of a node spread along the part of it its shape allows (16 points apart, closer when the side is short) and settled so that edges run straight, labels and bends brought into line with an end, nearly straight edges snapped, bends straightened and left out past labels; and each gap between ranks sized to the levels its curves turn onto, or the tracks of stepped edges. A flowchart's edges (an agent flow's and a flowchart's groups' included) meet their shapes where the layout left their ends, moved straight in onto the outline as Pele's `insetRoute` does (`FlowchartRouteEnds`); the other types still spread their ends themselves. The measurements are Pele's: 40 points between nodes, 48 between ranks, 18 and 12 points of padding around a node's text, text wrapped at 200 points and an edge's text seven eighths of the nodes'.

A state diagram is laid out the same way, with the measurements of Pele's state renderer: 44 points between ranks, 16 and 10 points of padding around a state's name, starts first in the order, then the states in the order transitions first name them. Transitions to a state itself loop beside it, and notes sit beside their state across the flow, a rank gap away; a state's place in its rank takes both on each side, so that its transitions still meet it in the middle.

Subgraphs are laid out as Pele's compound layout does, ported step for step from `layout/compound.ts` (`LayeredCompoundLayout`, each diagram writing its nodes and boxes straight into its spans as a `LayeredCompoundGraph`): each box on its own, in ranks, in its own direction, from the deepest up, then placed in the box around it as one node of its size, with 20 points of padding and a band for its title; boxes inside each other are lifted to the top level, and a flowchart's group that an edge crosses the border of runs in the direction of the box around it, as Pele's `inheritCrossedDirections` has it after Mermaid. An edge crossing a border passes through a port on the side facing where it goes, its side decided by ranking the box's parent first: a port ranked before or after the box's content on the sides its flow starts from or ends at, or straight across the flow from a node pinned first or last in its rank; outside, the edge is held at that port. Where every box runs in one direction and the graph has 2 to 400 ports and at most 500 edges, one flat layout of every node, each group kept together in its ranks, keys the orders of all the boxes, an independent ordering is kept instead when its drawn routes cross less, and the ports' order is refined from where the routes turn outside each box, kept when the routes then cross less (`LayeredRouteCrossings` counts the crossings of the routes as they are drawn). As in Pele, an edge between a box and something inside it runs straight from one center to the other, and a loop on a box starts at its middle.

## Where the scenes differ from Graphite's reading views

The scenes paint as Pele does, and as this package's renderers did before them. Graphite's SwiftUI reading views of 0.1.0 differ in five places, which change what Graphite shows once it paints the scenes; they are intended, not regressions:

- A sequence diagram's number badges, a class diagram's lines and marks, and a user journey's score line and faces are painted in the `line` role (Pele's `--_l`), where the reading views use the foreground color. Graphite's `line` is the foreground unless a theme sets `--pele-line`, so these show only with such a theme.
- A quadrant chart's quadrant labels are painted before its points, as Pele paints them; the reading view paints them after, so a point over a label covers it.
- On a sequence diagram's colored box or `rect`, the lines, heads and texts of the messages wholly on it, the titles of blocks on it and the texts of figures in the box are painted in Pele's dark or white text color, whichever contrasts more with the color (`SequenceTextInk`); the reading view kept the line and text colors there.
